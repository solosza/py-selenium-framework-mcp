**What we sell:** Software that makes AI do complex work the right way, every time.

**The problem:** AI tools are powerful but inconsistent. They improvise, skip steps, and produce unpredictable results. Companies can't trust AI with critical workflows.

## Key Concepts & Terminology

### Complete Glossary (Plain English)

**Core Concepts:**

| Term | What It Means | Real-World Analogy | Marketing Usage |
|------|---------------|-------------------|-----------------|
| **Execution Engine** | Software that enforces rules during a process. It doesn't suggest — it requires. | Like a factory assembly line that physically can't skip steps, vs. a checklist someone might ignore. | "Isagawa is an execution engine for AI workflows" |
| **Domain Expertise** | Deep knowledge of a specific field built up over years of experience. | A 20-year tax attorney vs. someone who just read the tax code. | "We encode domain expertise into software" |
| **Enforcement Layer** | The part of software that prevents AI from deviating from the correct process. | Like a spell-checker that won't let you send with errors, vs. one that just underlines them. | "Built-in enforcement — not suggestions" |
| **Workflow** | A sequence of steps to complete a business task. | Processing a mortgage application: verify income → check credit → appraise property → approve/deny. | "Complex workflows executed correctly" |
| **Guardrails** | Boundaries that keep AI on track and prevent mistakes. | Like bumpers in bowling — you can't throw a gutter ball. | "Guardrails for AI workflows" |
| **Human Escalation** | When AI recognizes it's uncertain and asks a human for help instead of guessing. | A good employee who says "I'm not sure, let me check" instead of making something up. | "Knows when to ask for help" |

**Technical Terms (For Context Only — Don't Use in Marketing):**

| Term | What It Means | Why You Might Hear It |
|------|---------------|----------------------|

| **DevOps** | Development Operations. The practice of automating software deployment and monitoring. | Another potential market for us. |
| **QA/SDET** | Quality Assurance / Software Development Engineer in Test. People who test software professionally. | Our primary initial market. |

## Appendix: Understanding the Product (Non-Technical)

*This section explains how our product actually works. Read this after you understand the market opportunity above.*

### What Isagawa Actually Is

Think of Isagawa as **a system that makes AI follow expert-level procedures perfectly, every time**.

It's like having a master craftsman's knowledge built into a robot. The robot doesn't improvise or cut corners — it physically can't deviate from the expert's method.

### The Components (Plain English)

Isagawa has four main parts that work together:

```
+------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Component-by-Component Explanation

**1. RAG Assistant (The Smart Guide)**

| What It Is | What It Does | Real-World Analogy |
|------------|--------------|-------------------|
| An AI helper trained on our expert knowledge | Answers questions, explains concepts, guides users through the process | Like a senior colleague who's always available to help and never gets annoyed at questions |

*"RAG" stands for Retrieval-Augmented Generation — it means the AI can look up information from our expert knowledge base instead of making things up.*

**2. Skills (The Instruction Manual)**

| What It Is | What It Does | Real-World Analogy |
|------------|--------------|-------------------|
| Step-by-step guides written by experts | Tells the AI exactly what to do and in what order | Like a detailed recipe from a master chef — follow it exactly and you get the same result every time |

*Skills are the "how to do it right" knowledge. They guide the AI through complex workflows step by step.*

**3. Quality Gates / MCP Tools (The Inspector)**

| What It Is | What It Does | Real-World Analogy |
|------------|--------------|-------------------|
| Automated checkpoints that verify work | Checks that each step was done correctly before allowing the next step | Like airport security — you can't proceed unless you pass inspection |

*This is the ENFORCEMENT layer. It's what makes Isagawa different from other AI tools. The AI literally cannot skip steps or do things wrong because the Quality Gates won't allow it.*

**4. Framework (The Building Blocks) — Tech Verticals Only**

| What It Is | What It Does | Real-World Analogy |
|------------|--------------|-------------------|
| Pre-built professional code patterns | Provides the actual structure for technical work (like test code) | Like pre-fabricated house components — professionally engineered, just needs assembly |

*The Framework is only needed for technical domains where we're generating actual code (like QA testing). Non-tech verticals don't need this.*

| **RAG Assistant** | Yes — guides technical process | Yes — guides domain process |
| **Skills** | Yes — 9-step test creation workflow | Yes — domain-specific workflow |

| **Output** | Professional test code | Correct process execution/documentation |

### Why This Matters for Marketing

**The core product is the same:**
- Skills (the expert knowledge)
- Quality Gates (the enforcement)
- RAG Assistant (the guide)

**What changes per vertical:**
- The specific rules being enforced
- Whether a Framework is needed (only for code-generating verticals)

**This means:**
- We can enter new verticals by creating new Skills + Quality Gates
- No need to rebuild the core product for each industry
- Each vertical is a new revenue stream
